FINTECH Circle Playbook

The webinar funnel from start to finish, how the Claude Project runs it, and the next steps

Version: 14 October 2026 For: Susanne Chishti and the operations team Contains: no passwords, keys or live figures

How it works

It works as a very detailed, trained chatbot. The team (and Susanne) type a question or a request in plain language in the Claude Project chat. - It is trained on the business: the playbook, rules, persona, voice guide and known traps are loaded as knowledge files, so answers follow how FINTECH Circle works. - It is connected to the live systems: it reads Tantra, Instantly, GoHighLevel, WebinarGeek and the outreach sheet directly, so numbers are read at the source each time. - It answers, and it can act: most of the time it answers. When asked to do a task it shows a plan first and waits for a typed "go", or "send now" for anything that reaches people. It never acts on its own.

How to use this document. Part A explains the business and the funnel. Part B is the webinar cycle step by step, with what to type in the Claude Project chat at each step. Part C is the reference: tools, automation, content, rules and traps. Part D is the next steps, including the move to GitHub and Claude Code. Live numbers (RSVPs, revenue, lead counts, campaign status) are never written here; the chat reads them from the source each time.

Part A. The business and the funnel

A1. What the funnel is for

FINTECH Circle runs a free live webinar for senior financial-services leaders who want board, non-executive, advisory or fractional roles in UK fintech. The webinar leads to an application, a call and, for those accepted, enrolment in the Board Programme.

  • Webinar headline in use: The Insider Roadmap to Access Top UK Fintech Board Seats in 2026
  • Who it is for ("UK Portfolio Finance Leader"): 15 to 30+ years in banking, insurance, asset management, fintech or top-tier consulting and audit. Current or former C-suite, board member, NED or partner, moving away from a full-time role.
  • Wants: paid part-time strategic work, a meaningful title, to give back, intellectual engagement.
  • Fears: regulatory and reputational risk, and paying for courses that do not lead to roles.
  • Core problem: UK board seats are mostly filled through networks and headhunter shortlists before they are advertised.
  • Who is at the centre: Susanne Chishti, Founder & Chair of FINTECH Circle, nearly three decades in financial services, FTSE board member, co-editor of the FINTECH Book and its five companion titles. Her voice and experience are the source of the content.

A4. How a request runs, and the three safety levels

  1. The team types the request in the chat.
  2. Claude reads the knowledge files (the rules and traps) and calls the connector tools (live data).
  3. Claude shows a plan card: what was found now, what it will change, what it will not touch, risks, rollback, how it will verify.
  4. The team replies "go" (level 2) or "send now" (level 3).
  5. Claude reports each figure from its own source, in the turn it read it.
Level Typed word What it covers
Read none Numbers, lists, status, previews. Changes nothing.
Change "go" Creating a draft, a tab, a tag, a broadcast, a template; editing a draft or paused campaign; blocking a domain.
Reaches people "send now" Sending invites, activating a campaign, enrolling contacts in a live workflow, registering a person, cancelling a wave, editing an active campaign. Irreversible, so never assumed.

Truth rules. (1) A claim about a live system is valid only in the turn it was read. (2) Vendor counters are not proof of delivery; outcomes are confirmed at the system that did the work. (3) Procedure lives in code, not in reminders; anything that says "remember to" is enforced by a tool instead.

Part B. The webinar cycle, step by step

Each step shows what happens, what to type, who gates it and what you get. Timing rule of thumb: calendar invites go out 3 to 7 days before the event, and results are judged at day 3 to 4, not day 1, because RSVPs accrue over time.

B1. Set up the webinar

What happens. Confirm the event exists in WebinarGeek and that the outreach sheet has a tab for it. Type: "New webinar on 16 October at 12:00. What exists already and what is missing?" 1. Claude finds the next broadcast on the existing webinar. If one exists at that time it is reused, never duplicated. 2. If it is missing, Claude shows a plan card and creates it on "go" (60-minute duration, London time). 3. Claude lists the sheet tabs. If the tab for this webinar is missing, it creates WEB #<n> <Mon D> on "go", copying the header row. 4. Claude checks unused leads on the tab and says plainly whether the pool is enough. You get: a confirmed event, a ready sheet tab, and a yes or no on whether the list is enough. Still by a person: WebinarGeek emails, reminder times, call-to-action buttons and polls (login-only tasks), the LinkedIn event, banners and deck dates.

B2. Get the leads in

A wave can only invite people who are already in the Master Outreach Sheet. There are three routes.

Route When What happens today
A. Use the pool already in the sheet The tab has unused leads Chat counts them and plans the wave. Works now.
B. A new list from the team The pool is short or a new buy arrives The team attaches the list in chat. Claude counts rows, finds duplicate and missing emails, checks industries and countries against the target, flags other clients' contacts and anyone suppressed or already contacted, then writes an operations brief. Operations cleans and loads the tab; the chat then sees the new count.
C. Cold email only Leads sit in any Google Sheet tab with a header row Share the sheet with the connector's Google account. Claude previews the leads, removes suppressed and already-contacted people, and loads them into Instantly on "go". Works now.

The standard cleaning rules. De-duplicate by email (lowercase, trimmed). Keep only the nine financial industries: Accounting, Banking, Capital Markets, Financial Services, Insurance, Investment Banking, Investment Management, Venture Capital & Private Equity, Management Consulting. Remove webinar subscribers, attendees, RSVP responders, applicants and paying customers. Strip contacts that belong to other clients. Normalise countries (England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland become United Kingdom).

Not yet in chat: adding lead rows to the sheet, searching the whole lead pool by country, title or headcount, and counting leads used across several webinars. These are the planned lead tools (Part D).

B3. Send the calendar invites (Tantra)

What happens. Tantra creates a Google Calendar event and adds the leads as attendees from 20 warmed mailboxes. Creating a calendar campaign is the send. There is no draft and no pause. Type: "Plan the next wave for the upcoming webinar." 1. Claude selects leads: unused only, never stamped on any tab, never contacted in Tantra or Instantly, minus RSVPs, attendees, suppressed people, blocked domains and excluded countries and titles. 2. Claude shows the plan card: counts per wave, the invite variation per wave, the schedule, the geo mix and why leads were skipped. 3. On "send now", Claude creates each campaign with the required settings, confirms each reads ready, then marks only those leads as used. 4. If any wave is not ready, Claude re-checks a few minutes later. Delivery truth comes from the calendar RSVPs and the next day's dashboard.

Settings enforced on every wave: balanced distribution across mailboxes; London time for the event; about 1,000 invites per campaign; WebinarGeek broadcast linked and yes/maybe auto-registered; invite location is always the registration link with its tracking tag, never the WebinarGeek page; one variationN tag; no first-name placeholders in titles; stale dates blocked. The five invite titles (V1 to V5) are fixed and never renumbered; their results are tracked and used to pick the next wave's mix. Fixing a mistake: wave_cancel removes a wave. It first says whether the invites are still scheduled (cancelling should stop them) or already delivered (cancelling cannot recall them). It needs "send now", and it leaves the leads stamped as used.

B4. Launch the cold email (Instantly)

Type: "Set up the Instantly campaign for the next webinar." 1. Claude lists campaigns (ids change every webinar) and reads the latest one. 2. It proposes the date and time replacements as a plan card. 3. On "go" it copies the campaign into a new draft; it refuses if any old date or time is left in the copy. 4. It previews the leads, with suppressed, blocked and previously contacted people removed, then loads them in batches on "go". 5. The team reviews the draft together. Claude can edit any step (subject, body, delay), the name, daily limit or end date; it shows the exact before and after and blocks stale dates. 6. The campaign starts only on "send now". Current pattern: two cold variants A/B-testing a Personal and a Value angle, plus a retarget campaign aimed at the previous webinar's yes/maybe no-shows. Trap: a campaign name can say "DRAFT" while its status is active. Trust the status field, never the name.

B5. Check the landing page and the automations

Before the invites land, confirm the registration page shows the new date and that the registration path works end to end. - The page reads the upcoming broadcast from the backend; verify the date shown. - Test one registration: a confirmation email arrives from WebinarGeek, and (if a phone number is given) the WhatsApp join-link and reminder path starts. - Timezone must agree in two places: the page and the Worker both use Europe/London. Nothing enforces this, so check it when the schedule changes. - Landing page changes: Claude writes a precise change request (page, section, old text to new text, and why). The operations team applies it, previews it, and publishes only after a test registration and a "go".

B6. What happens when someone registers (automatic)

  1. The registration form sends one submit to four places at once: WebinarGeek (the only one that can fail the registration), a Google Sheet, GoHighLevel, and the thank-you page.
  2. WebinarGeek sends the confirmation immediately and the reminders on schedule.
  3. The thank-you page asks the registrant to WhatsApp "send me my join link". That inbound message opens WhatsApp's 24-hour window, which is why the confirmation can be a free-form message. This ask is load-bearing; do not remove it.
  4. GoHighLevel replies with the join link, then sends four timed WhatsApp reminders anchored on the broadcast date. The morning reminder sits 4.5 hours before the broadcast.
  5. The Worker keeps GHL up to date from WebinarGeek events (registered, unsubscribed, watched live, watched replay, no-show) by setting state tags.
  6. A registrant without a phone number still gets the full WebinarGeek email journey; they just do not get WhatsApp.

B7. After the event

Type: "How did the webinar do?" · "Tag last webinar's yes and maybe no-shows for retargeting." - Attendance: registrations, unsubscribes, attendees and show rate, read from WebinarGeek per broadcast. - No-shows: the Worker's tags identify them; Claude tags them for retargeting on "go" (and first lists any published workflow the tag would trigger). Enrolling them in a live workflow, which can send WhatsApp or email, needs "send now". - Follow-up and replay go out as WebinarGeek email. There is no WhatsApp follow-up sequence. - Applications: applicants fill the form; Susanne sets the decision in the sheet; the email goes out automatically (see C5).

B8. Review and learn

  • Read RSVPs from the calendar (not Tantra's sent counter, which reads zero for calendar campaigns that did send).
  • Compare cold-email performance, bounce rates and revenue by tier.
  • Feed what worked (invite titles, countries, seniority) into the next wave's mix. Weak or high-bounce lists are not reused.

B9. Protect the list at any time

Type: "This company asked to be removed. Block them everywhere." Claude blocks the whole domain in Instantly, marks every record at that domain as unsubscribed in Tantra (contacts are duplicated per list, so every record is patched), and adds it to the shared suppression list. Every later lead preview then excludes them. It never blocks a free email provider or one of our own sending domains. Blocking is not erasure: a deletion request also needs the row removed from the sheets and a reply sent.

C3. Calendar and cold-email rules (breaking these breaks sends)

  • Calendar invites: balanced distribution, never fill-first; event time in London; about 1,000 per campaign; broadcast linked; auto-register on; location is the registration link; send 3 to 7 days before. Capacity is limited by the shared Google quota, about 1,500 invites per service account per day.
  • Creating a calendar campaign is the send. Only name and tags can be edited afterwards; to change anything else, cancel and recreate.
  • Cold email: one campaign per webinar named Webinar <n> - <D Month>; stays a draft until "send now"; ids change every webinar so always list.
  • A decline of a calendar invite is treated as an opt-out.

C4. Automation that is already running

  • Email (WebinarGeek): confirmation, reminders, follow-up, replay. WhatsApp (GHL): join link on request, four timed reminders.
  • Worker: POST /webinargeek/ghl verifies a signature, upserts the contact on email, fills the webinar fields, sets state tags. It reads state from the subscription data, not the event name, so a retried or dropped webhook cannot corrupt a record.
  • State tags the Worker sets: webinar registrant · webinar attended · live webinar watched · watched webinar replay · live webinar not watched · webinar unsubscribed · clicked webinar CTA. Post-webinar tags are cleared on re-registration so the next webinar re-fires workflows. Use only these names; older hyphenated duplicates are historical and must not be used in new filters.
  • Fields the reminders depend on: webinar_broadcast_date (the anchor), watch_link_webinargeek, readable_webinar_date, readable_webinar_time.
  • GHL workflows: 18 exist, 5 are published (the WhatsApp reminders, the inbound join-link reply, the CISI applicants flow, and two course-access flows). The draft webinar-email workflows must stay unpublished, because publishing one would double-send against WebinarGeek.
  • Daily dashboard: rebuilt automatically each day and published; a second check re-runs it if the day has no successful run.
  • Weekly deliverability report on the sending domains.

C5. Applications

Three application forms feed Google Sheets: our own, a partner (CISI) form with its own sheet and Calendly, and a future-cohort form. When Susanne sets Approval (column Q) to Accepted or Rejected, an installable trigger sends the matching email from boards@fintechcircle.com, BCCs the team and writes the send into column R. An hourly sweep catches anything missed. Any non-empty value in column R counts as handled, which prevents duplicate sends. Never re-run "Setup Spreadsheet" on a live sheet, and remember that saving an Apps Script is not deploying it.

C6. The content workflow

Susanne is the source; the team produces; Susanne approves. 1. Brief (the team): format, topic or Susanne's notes, source material, call to action (webinar or follow), date. 2. Claude drafts: post copy in her voice, first-comment text, slide-by-slide text with a layout note, a design brief naming the template. 3. Claude checks against the approval checklist and fixes any failure. 4. Susanne signs off every post. Recorded in the Content Calendar tab of the Work Tracker. 5. Design and schedule (the team): finished in the named template, scheduled in Metricool or LinkedIn.

Ten approved formats. Image: Experiential · Thought leadership · Quotes · Cheatsheet. Carousel: Framework deck · Comparison deck · Checklist deck · Illustrated story · Book or resource · Case study (real, permissioned cases only). Videos are out of scope for now.

What goes in (inputs). - Her voice: her tone-of-voice questionnaire (friendly, knowledgeable, positive; nothing negative or controversial); first person, authority lived and never claimed; structure of hook, tension, three numbered points, stakes, one call to action; her approved posts as the quality bar; British English. - Substance: her career and boards, the FINTECH Book series, the Board Programme and its nine modules, the webinar headline, testimonials only with permission. - Audience: the persona in A1. - Design: brand palette (ocean, navy, dusk, sky, sun), the identity chip, page numbers and footer logo on every slide, the craft rules, the named template per format, finished example carousels as structure references only. - CTA rules: webinar posts put the registration link in the first comment and end with "Link in the first comment." Follow posts end with the follow line. One call to action per post and the post ends on it.

Never: hype words, manufactured urgency, "comment and I'll DM", a closing question to the reader, invented statistics, clients or quotes, anything negative or critical, anything market-sensitive or confidential, investment advice, another client's content, the long dash (use a short hyphen), and the name of the listed bank she sits on.

Weekly routine: the team sends the brief → Claude returns the draft, already checked → Susanne approves or edits → the team designs and schedules → log it in Content Calendar. Target volume: [__ posts per week - to confirm].

C9. Known traps (where wrong answers come from)

  • Tantra under-reports outreach (deleted campaigns are invisible) and its sentCount is 0 for calendar campaigns. Use the calendar RSVPs.
  • Instantly analytics under-report replies badly and show 0 opens (tracking is off). Do not quote them as real.
  • Master sheets for early webinars are lead lists with an empty tracking scaffold: they show who was targeted, never what was sent.
  • Early "applications" and "paid customers" files are stale exports. Revenue comes live from GHL payments.
  • Some raw lists store first and last names swapped; cross-check against the email before trusting a first name.
  • A calendar campaign created without the broadcast link can never be linked afterwards (the field is create-only).
  • Never judge a wave on day 1; RSVPs accrue.

D2. Move the codebase to GitHub and add Claude Code

Why. Chat acts only when someone types. It cannot run on a timer or change code. For everything to be automated properly, the code must be in a shared, versioned home where Claude Code can work on it.

What Claude Code unlocks. Landing page edits (preview, then publish, with rollback) · scheduled jobs (daily dashboard, weekly deliverability report, a reply sweep before each wave) · building the lead tools in D1 · the unsubscribe sync between GHL and Instantly · reading calendar-invite replies · version-controlled outreach and email templates · reviewed, reversible changes.

Step by step

  1. Create the GitHub home. One FINTECH-owned GitHub account or organisation, with at least two owners (so access is never lost with one person), two-factor authentication on, and team members added by role. Decision: account or organisation, and who owns it.
  2. Create a private repository. Private always; one client per repository so data can never mix. Suggested name: fintech-circle-automation.
  3. Add the codebase. Suggested layout: - method/ : the playbook, scripts and templates that are client-agnostic - scripts/ : the wave, dashboard and monitoring scripts - connector/ : the Cloudflare Worker that powers the chat tools - mcp-server/ : the Tantra tool server - knowledge/ : the Claude Project knowledge files (00 to 20) and the documentation - monitoring/ : the deliverability reports - CLAUDE.md : the rules file Claude Code reads first (client identity, standing rules, the truth rules, the plan-card rule)
  4. Keep these OUT of the repository (enforced by a .gitignore and a pre-commit check): passwords and API keys (.env), service-account files and the secrets folder, raw and processed lead lists, engaged-people and suppression exports, generated state files and anything with personal data, .DS_Store and local caches. Secrets live only in each system's own settings (for example the Worker's secret store) and are never committed.
  5. Keep related repositories separate. The landing page and the technology ecosystem page already have their own repositories and stay there. A method-only skeleton repository (no client data) already exists and can seed the shared method.
  6. Add Claude Code. Open the repository in Claude Code with the same rules file and knowledge layer so it follows this playbook. Give it only the access it needs; secrets are supplied at run time, not stored.
  7. Work through changes. Every change goes on a branch, is reviewed, then merged. Nothing goes live from an unreviewed branch. Every change has a history and can be undone.
  8. Add automation. Scheduled jobs and checks run from the repository (for example daily and weekly jobs), with failures reported to the team.
  9. Document and hand over. A short README for the team: how to ask for a change, how a change is reviewed, how to roll back.

Guardrails

  • Private repository, team access only, two owners.
  • Secrets never enter the repository; a secret scan runs before every commit.
  • Anything live (campaigns, sends, GHL) still waits for a plan card and a typed "go" or "send now", even from Claude Code.
  • Previews before publishing; rollback is promoting the previous version.
  • One client per repository.

Decisions needed

  • GitHub account or organisation, and who owns it.
  • Who gets access, and at what level.
  • Which repository holds the landing page.
  • Whether the pool is stored in the cloud for chat search (D1).

E4. Glossary

  • Plan card: the reviewed summary Claude shows before any change.
  • Wave: one Tantra calendar campaign of about 1,000 invites.
  • Stamp: marking a lead as used in the outreach sheet, only after its campaign is confirmed ready.
  • Broadcast: one scheduled showing of the webinar in WebinarGeek.
  • Connector: the service that lets the chat read and act on the live systems.
  • Suppression list: everyone who must not be contacted, shared across channels.
  • Worker: the small cloud program that links WebinarGeek to GHL and keeps tags in sync.

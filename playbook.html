The Problem This Playbook Fixes

Here is the problem this fixes. The funnel is made of a dozen tools, three automation layers and two outreach channels, and the knowledge of how it fits together has lived in a few people's heads and a few scattered documents. When a person is added, a campaign is changed, or a number looks wrong, nobody has one place to look.

This playbook is that place. It explains each stage in plain language, then gives a task card for every step that has to be done, in the same shape every time: why it matters, what Claude does, what a person does, every step, what good looks like and the common mistakes. Where a step can be run by typing a request into the Claude Project chat, the exact request is given.

What you walk away with

  • A clear picture of the six stages, the tool behind each one, and which tool is the system of record for every number.
  • A step by step cycle for running a webinar, from picking the date to reading the results, with the safety level of every step.
  • A content engine for Susanne's LinkedIn presence that keeps her in the editor's chair and the production off her desk.
  • A funnel equation that says which number to fix first, and where each input is read.
  • The rules, traps and open decisions, written down once so nobody has to rediscover them.

Who this is for. Susanne, who needs to understand and approve; the operations team, who run the cycle; and anyone hired later, who needs to be productive in days. Where a step needs a person, the card says so. Where Claude can do it, the card shows the request to type.

The Lessons in This Section

  • One funnel, six stages. Everything is a step in one of them.
  • Every step has an owner and a safety level, so nothing that reaches people happens by accident.
  • Live numbers are never written in a document. They are read from the system that did the work, on the day.

The three safety levels

Every task card carries one of these on its first line. The level is a promise about what the step can do to a real person.

The Truth Rules
  1. A claim about a live system is valid only in the turn it was read. Re-read before saying a campaign is paused, a draft or finished.
  2. Vendor counters are not delivery. Confirm at the system that did the work: calendar RSVPs for invites, WebinarGeek for attendance, GHL payments for revenue.
  3. Procedure belongs in code, not in reminders. A step that says "remember to" is a bug waiting to happen, so it is enforced by a script or tool and this playbook points to the command.
  4. A dated document carries STATUS and AS OF on its first lines, and is corrected the moment the status changes.

The Funnel Equation

A funnel is a chain of rates multiplied together. If one is weak, it drags every later number down, and improving a strong stage changes almost nothing. The equation below names every link and says where it is read, so the next experiment goes to the weakest link and not the favourite one.

No rate is quoted here. They change with every webinar. Read each from the source shown, and quote the time it was read.

The whole funnel

Part 2

Plan the Event

From four weeks out: the date, the broadcast, the sheet tab, the copy and the checks that make every later step safe.

Before You Start

This part is done once per webinar, three to four weeks before the event. Nothing here reaches people, so most steps are reads or low-risk changes. It is also where most late errors are born, so it is worth doing in order.

2.1 Pick the Date From the Cohort Calendar

Owner Susanne and operationsTime 20 minSystem Consolidated DocumentSafety Read

Why It Matters

The webinar is not a standalone event. It feeds a cohort application deadline, so the date decides how long the follow-up window is. A webinar about ten days before month end gives the cohort emails time to run before applications close.

How It Works

What Claude Does
  • Reads the Consolidated Document tab "Monthly Webinars for April 2026 to April 2027" and the dashboard's list of past and planned webinars.
  • Finds the next webinar number: the last number in the Outreach_Webinars tab plus one.
  • Reports which cohort the date feeds and how many days of follow-up that leaves.
What You Do
  • Choose the date and the start time. The default is 12:00 Europe/London; some events run at 11:00.
  • Confirm which cohort deadline it feeds.

Every Step

  1. Ask the chat: "What is the next webinar number, which cohort does 13 November feed, and how many follow-up days does that leave?"
  2. Check the date does not clash with the previous webinar's follow-up sequence.
  3. Write the date, time and cohort in the change log for this event (see 13.5, the new-event checklist).
  4. Tell the operations team, so the broadcast and sheet tab are created (next card).

Done When The date, time, webinar number and cohort are recorded in one place.

What Good Looks Like

  • The date is chosen from the calendar, not from memory.
  • The time is stated in Europe/London, because that is the audience time zone.

Common Mistakes

  • Choosing a date and only later discovering it leaves three days of follow-up.
  • Stating a time without a time zone.

2.2 Create the Broadcast and the Sheet Tab

Owner Operations, ClaudeTime 10 minSystem WebinarGeek, Master Outreach SheetSafety Go

Why It Matters

Everything downstream hangs off two objects: the WebinarGeek broadcast, which is what people register for, and the sheet tab, which is the queue of who to invite. A missing or duplicated broadcast sends invites to the wrong event.

How It Works

What Claude Does
  • Looks up the latest broadcast on the existing webinar. If one already exists on that date, it is reused, never duplicated.
  • Shows a plan card, then on "go" creates the broadcast on webinar 575538 (60 minutes, Europe/London). A new webinar is never created.
  • Lists the sheet tabs, and on "go" creates WEB #<n> <Mon D> with the header row copied from the latest tab.
  • Counts unused leads on the tab and says plainly whether the pool is big enough.
What You Do
  • Type the request in the chat and read the plan card.
  • Reply "go" only if the card shows what is expected.

Every Step

  1. Type: "New webinar on 13 November at 12:00. What exists already and what is missing?"
  2. Read the plan card. It lists what it found (existing broadcast, tab, Instantly campaign, Tantra campaigns), what it will create, and what it will not touch.
  3. Reply "go". Claude creates the broadcast and the tab, then reads them back to verify.
  4. Note the broadcast id. It goes into the event configuration (card 2.5).

Done When One broadcast and one tab exist for the event, each verified by reading it back.

What Good Looks Like

  • The duration reads 60 minutes. A wrong duration makes every calendar entry and reminder wrong.
  • The tab header matches the previous tab: column A is the date, B the owner, C the sender, and lead data starts in column D.

Common Mistakes

  • Creating a new webinar instead of a new broadcast on 575538.
  • Editing the broadcast time after invites have gone out. Invites keep the old time.

2.3 Update the Page Copy, Banners and Deck

Owner OperationsTime 2 hoursSystem WebinarGeek, LinkedIn, CanvaSafety Person

Why It Matters

Anyone who clicks through from an invite sees the registration page, the WebinarGeek description, the LinkedIn event page and the banners. A stale date on any of them costs registrations and creates confusion on the day.

How It Works

What Claude Does
  • Drafts the updated text for each surface from the Consolidated Document tabs: WebinarGeek Description, WebinarGeek Page Banner, LinkedIn Event Description, LinkedIn Event Page Banner.
  • Updates banners and the deck through the Canva connector where it is connected.
What You Do
  • Paste the text into WebinarGeek and LinkedIn by hand, because those edits are login only.
  • Update the deck: dates, the cohort, the offer slide, and align the live DM slide numbers with the current deck.

Every Step

  1. Ask for the new copy: "Draft the WebinarGeek description and LinkedIn event text for the 13 November webinar."
  2. Paste the WebinarGeek description and replace the banner.
  3. Update the LinkedIn event page and banner. Treat it as a secondary channel (2.5 explains why).
  4. Open the deck. Change dates, the next cohort, and the offer slide. Check the three call-to-action slides still point to the right links.
  5. Tick each item in the new-event checklist as it is verified.

  • Every price matches the current list: Digital £999, Flagship £2,950, Elite £7,200.
  • The cohort wording matches the next cohort. The application form and older copy still mention a May cohort and need updating.

Common Mistakes

  • Leaving an old price from earlier copy in a description.
  • Updating the page but not the banner, so the image still shows the old date.

2.4 Check the Landing Page, the Replay and the Emails

How It Works

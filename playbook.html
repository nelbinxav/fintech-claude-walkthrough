The Problem This Playbook Fixes

Here is the problem this fixes. The funnel is made of a dozen tools, three automation layers and two outreach channels, and the knowledge of how it fits together has lived in a few people's heads and a few scattered documents. When a person is added, a campaign is changed, or a number looks wrong, nobody has one place to look.

This playbook is that place. It explains each stage in plain language, then gives a task card for every step that has to be done, in the same shape every time: why it matters, what Claude does, what a person does, every step, what good looks like and the common mistakes. Where a step can be run by typing a request into the Claude Project chat, the exact request is given.

What you walk away with

  • A clear picture of the six stages, the tool behind each one, and which tool is the system of record for every number.
  • A step by step cycle for running a webinar, from picking the date to reading the results, with the safety level of every step.
  • A content engine for Susanne's LinkedIn presence that keeps her in the editor's chair and the production off her desk.
  • A funnel equation that says which number to fix first, and where each input is read.
  • The rules, traps and open decisions, written down once so nobody has to rediscover them.

Who this is for. Susanne, who needs to understand and approve; the operations team, who run the cycle; and anyone hired later, who needs to be productive in days. Where a step needs a person, the card says so. Where Claude can do it, the card shows the request to type.

The Lessons in This Section

  • One funnel, six stages. Everything is a step in one of them.
  • Every step has an owner and a safety level, so nothing that reaches people happens by accident.
  • Live numbers are never written in a document. They are read from the system that did the work, on the day.

The three safety levels

Every task card carries one of these on its first line. The level is a promise about what the step can do to a real person.

LevelTyped wordWhat it covers
ReadnoneNumbers, lists, status, previews, drafts. Changes nothing.
Go"go"Creating a draft, a tab, a tag, a broadcast, a template. Editing a draft or paused campaign. Blocking a domain.

The Truth Rules
  1. A claim about a live system is valid only in the turn it was read. Re-read before saying a campaign is paused, a draft or finished.
  2. Vendor counters are not delivery. Confirm at the system that did the work: calendar RSVPs for invites, WebinarGeek for attendance, GHL payments for revenue.
  3. Procedure belongs in code, not in reminders. A step that says "remember to" is a bug waiting to happen, so it is enforced by a script or tool and this playbook points to the command.
  4. A dated document carries STATUS and AS OF on its first lines, and is corrected the moment the status changes.

The Funnel Equation

A funnel is a chain of rates multiplied together. If one is weak, it drags every later number down, and improving a strong stage changes almost nothing. The equation below names every link and says where it is read, so the next experiment goes to the weakest link and not the favourite one.

No rate is quoted here. They change with every webinar. Read each from the source shown, and quote the time it was read.

  14. Credentials and data. A password is given only in a direct chat when the operator asks for a tool's login or is being walked through a UI-only task. Other Funnel Shapes

The three shapes in depth, seventeen more at a glance with a fit rating for FINTECH, and seven small tests worth running.

The Four Jobs of Any Funnel

FINTECH Circle runs one funnel shape today: the webinar. It is the right first shape, because one live session shows which message lands. But it is not the only shape, and a funnel that has a clear weakest link may be better fixed by changing the shape than by tuning it. This part explains the shapes that exist, and which are worth testing next. It is the "what else could we run" part of the playbook.

The four jobs of any funnel. Whatever the shape, a funnel does the same four jobs. What changes is the moment and the format in which the sale is asked for.

  1. Convert a completely cold audience. Named people who have never heard of FINTECH Circle are reached directly and given a reason to look. This is the calendar invite and cold email.
  2. Generate traffic from many sources. Outreach, content, ads and partners all point at one place, so no single platform can starve the funnel.
  3. Give value first. A live session, a video or a workshop day gives real value before anyone asks for money. For this audience, an insider view of how board searches really run is the value.
  4. Keep delivering value for the whole journey. Reminders, follow-ups, a newsletter and a community keep the relationship warm long after the first touch.

And there is a door at every stage: a person ready to apply, book a call or buy can do so at the stage they are in, not only at the end.

14.1 Write the Funnel Spec

Owner Operations, ClaudeTime 2 hoursSystem Consolidated Document, repositorySafety Go

Why It Matters

Facts that live in five places drift. A price worded one way on the page and another in an email goes live unnoticed. The fix is one file that holds every fact once, and one rule: change the spec first, never the tool directly. From then on, the page, the emails and the campaigns copy from the spec.

How It Works

What Claude Does
  • Reads every knowledge file and asks only about what is missing or contradicts itself.
  • Writes the spec and restates it for approval line by line, before touching anything live.
What You Do
  • Answer from what is true today, not what is hoped. "I do not know yet" beats a made-up answer.
  • Correct the restated spec, then approve it.

Every Step

  1. Ask: "Read every knowledge file. Ask me only about what is missing or contradicts itself. Then write the funnel spec with each price, date, link, promise, cohort and time once, and restate it for my approval."
  2. Answer the questions. The usual conflicts are the cohort date, the module count and an old price.
  3. Read the restated spec hunting for the same fact worded two ways.
  4. Approve. Record the spec's place in the Consolidated Document, with a status line.

14.10 Set Guardrails for Unattended Running

WhatsApp Automation in Detail

The design trick. The thank-you page asks the registrant to WhatsApp the words "send me my join link". That inbound message opens WhatsApp's 24-hour customer-service window. That is why the confirmation can be a free-form message instead of a paid template, and why the reminders that follow land reliably. The ask on the thank-you page is load-bearing.

Workflow 1: Reply to Inbound WhatsApp Messages

  • Trigger: customer replied, filtered to WhatsApp messages. Match: the message contains "send me my join link".
  • Wait 0.2 minutes, so the reply does not arrive before their message has settled.
  • Guard: an If/Else on the three join-link fields joined with OR. The send happens only when all three are populated, so nobody receives a blank join link.
  • Send: a WhatsApp message with the join link.
  • Rough edge: a contact who fails the guard exits silently, with no notification and no fallback. The undelivered, STOP and time-out branches are empty.

Workflow 2: The Reminder Workflow

  • Trigger: a tracking event from the registration form on the landing page. First check: the contact has a phone number.
  • Waits are wait-until steps anchored on webinar_broadcast_date. Four reminders, each an approved WhatsApp template.
  • The morning reminder is 4 hours 30 minutes before the broadcast (06:30 for an 11:00 webinar). It was 6 hours (05:00) and was changed deliberately.
  • Structural risk: offsets are relative, so an unusually early or late broadcast shifts every reminder, including into unsociable hours. Look at it whenever the schedule changes.

WhatsApp follow-up is not implemented. After the webinar, follow-up is WebinarGeek email only. To add WhatsApp follow-up: a wait node at the end of the reminder workflow, then follow-up templates over the next two to three days.

The Phases and the Rule That Keeps Them Reusable

This part is the method that built the sending estate and that extends or repairs it. It is client-agnostic: the same phases apply to any funnel, and each client's own values live in its configuration file and knowledge folder, never in a script. For FINTECH Circle the estate already exists, so this part matters in three situations: adding capacity (a new domain, mailbox or channel), replacing something that has worn out, and handing the system to someone new.

The phases, in order. Each gates the next.

  1. Accounts, access and billing.
  2. Sending infrastructure: domains, authentication, mailboxes, warm-up.
  3. LinkedIn accounts and outreach (only if a LinkedIn channel is added).
  4. Persona, ICP and the lead pool.
  5. Webinar setup and tools.
  6. Content and creative.
  7. Campaign creation.
  8. Running and daily operations.
  9. CRM, measurement and the post-webinar cycle.

Sheet ids, impersonate-subjects, key paths, geographies and industries live in each client's client.config and .env, never baked into a shared script. One script, any client, zero risk of pointing at the wrong account. The working folder is the boundary: a session in one client's folder cannot touch another's data.   7. Verify: GET /accounts in Tantra shows the expected count connected on this client's service account, with the right warm status and daily maximum.

Done When Warm-up is running, limits are set in configuration, and the connected sender count matches expectation.

What Good Looks Like

  • To send more, add mailboxes. Never raise a per-account cap.

Common Mistakes

  • Reading the mailbox limits as the cap when the campaign's own limit is lower.

16.6 Approve the Persona and ICP in Writing

Owner Susanne, operationsTime 1 daySystem Persona document, data vendorSafety Person

Why It Matters

A data vendor scrapes against an ICP the client has approved. That ICP is a commercial commitment, not a working note: an unapproved or sloppy one buys the wrong list at the client's expense, and the invoice is per list, so a rebuilt list is a second invoice.

Every Step

  1. The questionnaire: what the company sells and to whom, the problem the buyer already knows they have, target industries, titles and seniority, geographies, company size, named accounts to include, accounts and competitors to exclude, and existing customer and lead lists to suppress. Exclusions are the field most often left blank and the most expensive to miss.
  2. The persona call: pressure-test three things. Does the title list exist in the tools, or is it a real job and a near-empty filter? Is the geography the buying geography or the headquarters geography? Is the seniority the buyer or the signer? Measure per client which converts.
  3. Write the persona document and the machine-readable slice for client.config (target industries, geographies, seniority, headcount) so every script filters the same way.
  4. Get written approval. Record the date, who approved and the exact ICP text. When a list comes back wrong, the first question is always which ICP it was built from.
  5. Send the ICP to the vendor. They check the available total in Apollo or ZoomInfo and report it before scraping. Read that number against the send plan: working days to the webinar times daily sending capacity is how many contacts the build can use. A total far below it means the ICP is too narrow, so widen it before paying. A total far above it means buy in tranches.
  6. Record per list: vendor, date, the ICP it was built from, row count, source tool and the invoice amount.

Done When A written, dated approval on file for every list, and a record of what each list cost and where it came from.

What Good Looks Like

  • Geography is re-derived from the data, never read off the configuration. A stale configuration line misleads for months.

Common Mistakes

  • Sending the ICP to the vendor before the written approval.

16.7 Clean, Validate and Apply the Exclusion Stack

Owner OperationsTime 1 to 2 days per listSystem Cleaning pipelineSafety Go

Why It Matters

No list enters a campaign unvalidated. One bad list spikes the bounce rate and can damage several sending accounts at once. Nothing in the tooling forces this, so the operator is the gate.

Every Step

  1. Keep the vendor file untouched in the raw sources folder. It is the receipt for what was paid for. Never edit it in place.
  2. Dedupe by lowercased email across every pool the client has, not only the new one. The overlap between vendor deliveries is routinely large.
  3. Drop rows with no email or no first name, because personalisation fails loudly on them. Segregate into the slices campaigns will use, by geography (check the email domain, not only the country column), persona tier and channel eligibility.
  4. Reconcile the row count against the invoice. A short delivery is a conversation with the vendor that must happen before use.
  5. Validate in four layers, cheapest first: syntax, metadata (free provider, disposable, role account), DNS (does the domain publish MX), then SMTP. A syntax or disposable failure stops. The result is reachable yes (the only positive confirmation), reachable no (remove it) or reachable unknown (the check could not find out: not the same as bad). A yes is a point in time, so re-verify before a large send. Validate the pool you are about to send to, not the slice that was easy to validate.
  6. Apply the exclusion stack in this order: the do-not-contact list (unconditional); everyone already in Tantra, from a freshly exported snapshot; webinar registrants and attendees; RSVP responders from prior calendar sends; bounces and unsubscribes; region and persona filters.
  7. Re-export the contacted snapshot immediately before every build. A snapshot taken before the last lists existed re-offers the leads you just invited. This is the most repeated mistake.
  8. Name every data file with its row count, for example (51,060).csv. If the count changes, rename the file. A count that lies is worse than none.

Done When A list that is deduped, validated, excluded against a fresh snapshot, and reconciled to the invoice.

What Good Looks Like

  • Zero overlap between batches, checked by joining on email and not by trusting the builder.

Common Mistakes

  • Sizing a pool from the contacts total. It counts list memberships, not unique people.
  • A channel that predates the current tooling is invisible to the snapshot. Any such channel is a hole in the exclusion stack.
  • Suppressing an address in one system and assuming it is suppressed everywhere. Tantra's unsubscribe works only on contacts already imported. Exclude by email at build time.
  • Trying to remove someone from a Tantra list. Membership cannot be edited. Build a new list. The original must never be attached to a campaign.

16.8 Write the Copy Under the Rules

Owner Operations, ClaudeTime 1 daySystem Instantly, Tantra, Consolidated DocumentSafety Go

Why It Matters

Each copy rule below was learned from something that went wrong. They are checked on the live payload, not by reading the draft.

Every Step

  1. Zero long dashes, verified by counting characters in the live payload.
  2. Only the tokens that actually resolve. Cross-platform token syntax does not transfer. Send yourself a test and confirm.
  3. Fallbacks drop the name mid-sentence, never substitute a placeholder.
  4. An unsubscribe line in every cold email, verified on every step, not just step one.
  5. Every URL UTM-tagged. Zero untagged URLs is a verification criterion.
  6. Follow-up steps carry no subject, so they thread as replies to step one.
  7. A/B tests need matched audiences or they prove nothing: same title, same event, same list construction, zero overlap, sent on consecutive days. Compare registrations by send date, not by total. Rotate copy across waves and choose on evidence.
  8. Verify the copy went out as written. Every calendar campaign is checked against the template live: character count, link count and dash count. A campaign cloned from another does not necessarily carry the same description. Diff it first.

Done When The live campaign matches the template, with tokens resolving and every link tagged.

What Good Looks Like

  • Sequence shapes in use: cold email in several steps a few days apart with an A/B subject only on step one; a single calendar invite where the description is the whole message; a LinkedIn sequence with delays measured from the accept.

Common Mistakes

  • Trusting the payload you sent and not the live campaign.
  • A cloned campaign carrying an older description.

16.9 Pass the Pre-Launch Gate

Owner OperationsTime 30 minSystem All channelsSafety Send now

Why It Matters

Creating a Tantra campaign sends, with no draft. Instantly and LinkedIn campaigns are drafts that need an explicit start. Three platforms, two different send semantics. Know which one you are in, and block the launch if any of the checks below fails.

Every Step

  1. Plan the volume first. Count working days to the webinar, divide the audience by them for the daily volume, distribute across accounts within their limits, and record date, owner and leads used for every batch. If the arithmetic does not fit the limits, the answer is more inboxes or an earlier start, never a higher per-account cap.
  2. Dry run first, always, and read the whole banner: contact count, event time, description length, sender count, and the headroom line, which catches a mailbox whose remaining quota is being used by something else.
  3. Pin senders explicitly. A clone of an older campaign lists that campaign's senders, which may have been disconnected since. Rotate sender sets between days.
  4. After a create, verify every time: creationStatus: ready, not paused, campaignDays is 1 unless a multi-day spread is intended, and events times guests equals the list size.
  5. Dispatch has taken up to 13 minutes to start, and RSVPs read zero the whole time. Do not conclude a send has failed at five minutes, and do not create a replacement: that is how duplicates happen.
  6. A scheduled campaign can show an event error in the Tantra interface despite a clean status. Re-check after the scheduled time passes.
  7. The gate: every sending account passed warm-up and every domain passes authentication; the list is validated, deduped and excluded; zero overlap with any other list in flight; copy verified live; limits respected; senders pinned and re-read immediately before start; the row map written and ready to stamp; a test send received and read in a real inbox; every link in the sequence opened in a real browser, including the booking link, which must load and show bookable slots.

Done When Every item passes. If any fails, the launch is blocked.

What Good Looks Like

  • The booking link is tested by booking a test slot. A dead booking link makes the funnel end in nothing.

Common Mistakes

  • Running a create exploratorily. Dry run first, always.

16.10 Run the Daily Loop

Owner OperationsTime 20 min a daySystem Dashboard, sheet, toolsSafety Go

Why It Matters

Deliverability is maintained continuously, not periodically. An account that underperforms despite warm-up and clean lists drags down the accounts around it, and a campaign that keeps sending while deliverability degrades burns the inboxes needed for the next one.

Every Step

  1. Regenerate live state and read it. Never answer a question about a campaign from prose in a file.
  2. Read the dashboard and check the generated time.
  3. Check account health across every channel: bounces, failures, disconnections.
  4. Send the day's batch through the tools (Part 4).
  5. Stamp the sheet after dispatch is confirmed, never before. A row stamped for a campaign that never sent falsely claims those people were contacted, and every future build will skip them. The next build refuses to run while any batch is unstamped.
  6. Pace and rotate. Respect the caps, rest a sender set for about two days between uses, and scale in steps, never in jumps. A sudden volume spike is the clearest signal to a spam filter.
  7. Keep the lists clean. Remove bounced and invalid addresses promptly: bounce rate is the fastest way to damage a reputation. Every unsubscribe, bounce and do-not-contact request goes into the do-not-contact file and is suppressed in each live system.
  8. If deliverability slips, pause or adjust. Do not push through. Replace what is failing.

Done When State read, health checked, the day's batch sent and stamped, and any failing account dealt with.

What Good Looks Like

  • A silently stale dashboard is the worst failure mode: it looks current and is believed. Alert on failure by email. If the pipeline can fail without anyone hearing, assume it already has.

Common Mistakes

  • A dashboard that refreshes on a laptop that is asleep.
  • A vendor starting to block the fetch because of the default user agent. Set an explicit one.

16.11 Use the Reporting Language Rules

Owner EveryoneTime Standing ruleSystem ReportsSafety Read

Why It Matters

Two rules about words, because the wrong word produces the wrong decision.

Every Step

  1. RSVPs are never registrations. Report WebinarGeek subscribers. A registrant is someone who filled in a form. A calendar Yes or Maybe bridged into the broadcast counts as interest, and the funnel equation shows auto-registrants separately for that reason. Do not quote an RSVP-to-registration rate.
  2. Vendor counters are not delivery. Confirm outcomes at the system that did the work. Tantra reports a sent count of zero on campaigns that provably sent, so calendar invites are verified by reading the sending mailboxes' calendars.
  3. Quote the generated or read time with every figure.
  4. A zero is a measured zero. A blank or a dash means not available.

Done When Reports use these words, and name which of three customer counts is meant.

What Good Looks Like

  • Every figure is attributed to the system that produced it.

Common Mistakes

  • Presenting a bridged RSVP as a registration.

16.12 Audit the CRM Before Anyone Reports From It

Owner OperationsTime 1 hourSystem GHLSafety Read

Why It Matters

Three defects are common in any CRM scaffold. Assume they exist until checked, because reporting from a CRM that has them gives confident wrong numbers.

Every Step

  1. Attendance tags under-count. Some attendees keep a registrant tag despite having watched, or a tag from the previous webinar. Filtering by tag misses people. The custom fields from WebinarGeek are the reliable signal, and tags are not.
  2. Two writers can use different formats for the same field. A timestamp or a yes value in two formats means anything that parses it must handle both.
  3. The lifecycle field is never populated. A status field that is empty for every attendee, a pipeline with no opportunities, and a source field that is blank on most records means the scaffold is not a CRM until someone fills it.
  4. There is no reliable revenue in the opportunity pipeline. Revenue is read from payment transactions (9.6).
  5. Do not "correct" GHL watch links from the WebinarGeek interface. Close the cycle: add the next webinar's tab, rebuild the pool and start again.

Done When The three defects are checked and any found are recorded.

What Good Looks Like

  • Webinar engagement fields keep a consistent suffix so they stay filterable as a group.

Common Mistakes

  • Building a re-invite audience from the watched flag. It is not the no-show list.

16.13 Add a LinkedIn Outreach Channel (If Wanted)

Owner Susanne, operationsTime 1 to 2 weeksSystem LinkedIn, outreach toolSafety Go

Why It Matters

FINTECH uses LinkedIn for content, an event page, forms and ads today, not for automated outreach. The call mentioned LinkedIn connection requests and messages as a later option inside Tantra. If it is added, these structural facts apply, and each one cost someone a rebuild.

Every Step

  1. Use the sender's own account, connected by them. The operator writes the connection steps and never holds the credentials.
  2. One campaign per sender voice. A campaign cannot have per-sender copy, and its lead list cannot be changed after creation. A second voice means a second campaign and a second list. Plan the voice split before building lists.
  3. Token syntax is specific to the tool. Prove it with a seed test, not by reading documentation.
  4. The delay after a not-accepted branch is the acceptance deadline. A short wait means a lead who accepts later never gets the sequence. Set it in days. The effective window is also capped by the request's auto-withdrawal.
  5. A campaign's sender list can drift. Re-read it immediately before starting.
  6. The daily connection-request ceiling observed is about 20, not 25. Plan to it. Sending hours are an account-level setting, not controlled by the copy.
  7. The lead list is append-only, so get it right before creation. Build as a draft and start only on a typed word.

Done When A tested, draft LinkedIn campaign with a recorded sender and voice, started only on "send now".

What Good Looks Like

  • LinkedIn already drives registrations through content. Outreach should not harm the account that carries the content.

Common Mistakes

  • Retrofitting a second voice into an existing campaign.

16.14 Point Every Sending Domain at a Real Page and Check Its Reputation

Owner OperationsTime 1 hour per batch of domainsSystem Cloudflare, Google Postmaster ToolsSafety Go

Why It Matters

A person who receives a cold message often types the sender's domain into a browser. A parked or error page looks like spam, and some filters check. Sending domains are separate from the main brand by design, so each one needs somewhere sensible to land. This card also holds the reputation checks that come before any scale-up.

How It Works

What Claude Does
  • Runs the pre-flight check on a name, lists the existing records and writes the redirect plan card.
  • Reads the weekly deliverability report and flags a domain whose authentication or blacklist status changed.
What You Do
  • Approve the plan card. Add the domains to Google Postmaster Tools, which needs a person with access to the domain.

Every Step

  1. Use separate sending-brand domains, never the main brand, each with a few mailbox subdomains such as team., events. and info..
  2. Redirect each domain. Add a 301 redirect rule in Cloudflare for the apex, www and the subdomains to the real webinar page.
  3. Do not leave the registrar's parking address as the origin. Point the record at a placeholder address that cannot answer (192.0.2.1, proxied) so the redirect fires at Cloudflare's edge and the parking page is never reached.
  4. Check authentication on every mailbox: SPF includes the sender, DKIM exists for each mailbox subdomain, and DMARC exists at the domain root. A subdomain with no record of its own inherits the root's policy (11.5).
  5. If DMARC reports flood an inbox, either remove the report address tags (enforcement stays, the mail stops) or point them at a free aggregator dashboard. Do not remove them if the reports are needed to raise enforcement safely.
  6. Check blacklists before scaling: Spamhaus DBL, SURBL and URIBL should be clean.
  7. Sending through Google Workspace uses Google's shared pool of addresses. There is no dedicated address to list, and the pool is not controllable per domain.
  8. Add each domain to Google Postmaster Tools to watch real domain reputation and spam rate.
  9. Keep the weekly audit running (11.6) with a notification on failure.

Done When Every sending domain lands on the real page, authenticates, is blacklist-clean and is watched.

What Good Looks Like

  • Typing a sending domain into a browser shows the webinar page.

Common Mistakes

  • Leaving the registrar's parking page live.
  • Removing the report tags and then raising DMARC enforcement blind.

16.15 Pace the Send Under the Real Ceiling and Judge at Day 3 to 4

Owner OperationsTime Each waveSystem Tantra, Instantly, Google CalendarSafety Go

Why It Matters

Most sending mistakes are pacing mistakes. Mailboxes that share one Google service account share one Calendar quota, so the real daily limit is far below mailboxes times the per-mailbox cap. A rule of thumb from this estate is roughly 750 to 1,500 invites a day through one service account, whatever the tool's headline capacity says. The other costly mistake is judging a wave on its first day.

How It Works

What Claude Does
  • Plans each wave against the service-account ceiling and the audience's local time, and flags a wave that exceeds it (the wave script has no daily-limit guard, 16.5).
  • Reads results at day 3 to 4 from the calendars, not the vendor counter.
What You Do
  • Decide how many days to spread a large send over. Approve the plan card. Say "send now".

Every Step

  1. Respect the ceiling. Do not schedule more per day than one service account can deliver. Spread across more days, or add service accounts in separate cloud projects (about one per thousand a day) if volume must rise.
  2. Use balanced distribution. Fill-first maxes the first accounts, so the next same-day campaign finds them full and sends nothing.
  3. Set the event time in the audience's time zone (Europe/London), never the operator's.
  4. Send three to seven days before the event.
  5. Choose slots in the recipient's local time. Tuesday to Thursday are best. Avoid Monday morning and Friday afternoon. Useful slots are 07:30 to 08:30, 11:00 to 12:00, 13:30 to 14:30 and 16:00 to 17:00.
  6. Test copy by splitting the list into equal random samples, one per campaign, with several titles and one constant description. Rotate the titles across time slots in a Latin-square pattern so no title is stuck in a good or bad slot. Equal volume per title gives a clean read.
  7. Keep personalisation out of the invite unless asked. It avoids merge errors.
  8. Test-render one invite and one email to yourself. The vendor preview can flatten line breaks that the delivered invite shows correctly, so only a real test settles it.
  9. After launch, watch for a large Not Sent count. It means the ceiling was hit and the invites never went out, which silently kills response. Pause and re-pace.
  10. Do not judge on day 1. RSVPs and replies accrue all week. Re-judge at day 3 to 4, and log the result in the campaign log for that run.

Done When The wave was paced under the ceiling, tested to the sender, read at day 3 to 4 and logged.

What Good Looks Like

  • A wave log shows the date, volume, title, slot and the day 3 to 4 read.

Common Mistakes

  • Reading the Tantra sent counter as delivery. It is always zero for calendar campaigns (10.1).
  • Calling a title a loser after one day.

16.16 Use the Cold-Outreach Benchmarks Only as a Starting Line

Owner OperationsTime 15 minutesSystem DashboardSafety Read

Why It Matters

Before a funnel has its own history, a rough benchmark tells you whether a number is in the right region. Once it has history, its own numbers replace the benchmark. These are method defaults, not FINTECH results.

Every Step

  1. Calendar outreach to senior executives: acceptance of about 0.2 to 0.3 percent, and total response (Yes, Maybe and No) of about 2 to 3 percent at maturity. Volume, not just rate, drives the absolute numbers.
  2. Webinar funnel conversions when there is no history: invites to page visits about 20 percent, visits to registrants about 40 percent, registrants to attendees about 50 percent, attendees to calls about 25 percent, calls to customers about 20 percent.
  3. Replace each benchmark with the funnel's own rate from the dashboard as soon as it has two or three events, and read it with the funnel equation in Part 1.
  4. The growth cadence. Run the live event with roles assigned, hold the sync call, hold the improvement call to analyse where people leaked, choose one hypothesis for one lever, log it in the tracker and repeat.

Done When Each number has a benchmark or its own history beside it, and the source is named.

What Good Looks Like

  • The first webinar is judged against a benchmark and the sixth against its own past.

Common Mistakes

  • Treating a benchmark as a target.
  • Comparing an early FINTECH number to a benchmark from a different audience.

17.13 How This Playbook Stays Complete

A playbook is complete only if something stops it going stale. These are the controls, and each one is a check, not a promise.

  1. One register. The coverage register above lists every source. A new source gets a row before it is used. A row with no section is a gap, and the pending list below is generated from those rows.
  2. Automated checks on every build. The build refuses to finish if any card is missing one of its required parts (why it matters, steps, what good looks like, common mistakes), if a cross-reference points nowhere, if a registered section id does not exist, or if a long dash appears.   7. A quarterly sweep. Re-read every dated block, re-run the checks, update the version date, and mark superseded files by renaming them.

Provenance Badges and the Honesty Rules
